# 1. Story and Setting

## 1.1 Setting

The setting is an Earth-like planet in a pre-industrial age, in the spirit of the
*Thousand and One Nights* and the Silk Road. Caravans cross deserts between oasis cities,
domed caravanserais mark each day's journey, and walled gardens, bazaars, citadels and
weavers' looms fill the settled lands. Beyond them lie steppes, jungles, frozen north
lands and seas, all shaped by the world's simulated history (see
[World history](10-world-history.md)). Most people are farmers, herders, traders and
craftsfolk. Mages are rare, often feared, sometimes licensed by rulers and sometimes
hunted by temple orders, and they usually work alone. Magic is real and powerful, but the
ley lines that carry mana have been weakening for ages. Few people know this, and fewer
care.

About forty years ago, seven mages formed the **Circle of the Loom**. Together they wove
the seven **Great Weaves**, artifacts that each bind a strand of the planet's ley network.
The flying carpet is one of them: the *Wandering Weave*. The Circle broke apart. Its
members scattered, hid their Weaves, and most of them grew old in obscurity.

The player's father, **Farrokh**, was the youngest member of the Circle. He settled in
**Anarvan**, a pomegranate-growing oasis village in the foothills between high mountains
and a great desert, fed by an ancient qanat. He became its master carpet weaver,
married, and raised his children. He hid the Wandering Weave in the most obvious place of all:
among the dozens of carpets in a weaver's house. His books and tools went into a sealed
cellar beneath the workshop.

## 1.2 The protagonist

- The player chooses a girl or a boy (default names *Nasrin* / *Kamran*, renameable),
  about 11 years old at the start, and a weaver's child who knows patterns, dyes and
  knots.
- **Co-op:** with up to four players, each player is one of Farrokh's children, the
  **heirs** of the Wandering Weave (siblings aged about 9 to 14, twins possible). Heirs
  who join later in the campaign were away from home when Anarvan burned, and they are
  reunited with the others through a story beat (see [Co-op](11-co-op.md)).
- The protagonist is physically weak. They can't wear armour or win a sword fight. Their
  strength is curiosity, stealth and a growing talent for magic.
- **Optional aging:** game time passes in seasons and years. The protagonist ages visibly
  (11 to roughly 15 over a typical playthrough). Aging gives small physical gains such as
  carry weight and stamina, and changes how NPCs speak to them. It is cosmetic-plus, never
  a fail state. Aging is on by default and can be switched off at world creation.

## 1.3 Prologue: "Anarvan" (tutorial, 20–40 minutes)

1. **Village life.** The player wakes up and does chores. They fetch water at the qanat
   outlet, feed the goats, pick pomegranates and herbs, and tie a few knots at the loom,
   which quietly introduces patterns as a theme. Talking to villagers teaches walking,
   interacting, picking things up and the basic inventory. Farrokh shows small "tricks",
   such as lighting an oil lamp with a word. He passes them off as a weaver's sleight of
   hand.
2. **The arrival.** At dusk, a mage on a carpet of his own dark weaving descends on the
   village. This is **Azhdar, the Ash Weaver**. He demands "the Weave", and the villagers
   don't understand. The tension rises.
3. **The handover.** Farrokh pulls one plain-looking rug from the stack in the workshop,
   presses it into the child's arms with a small leather bag, and says: *"Hide. Don't come
   out, whatever you hear."* He opens the hidden cellar hatch beneath the loom and closes
   it over the child. In co-op, all of his children present go into the cellar
   together.
4. **The hiding** (a short, tense stealth section with no fighting possible). Through
   cracks in the floor the player hears and sees fragments (in co-op, each heir watches
   through a different crack and sees different fragments): fire, screams, Farrokh's
   magic flaring briefly. Azhdar burns carpet after carpet looking for the Weave, and his
   search turns the village into ash. He senses that the Weave is close but can't find
   it, because the cellar is warded. He leaves in fury.
5. **Ashes.** Morning comes and the player climbs out. The village is destroyed and
   everyone is dead. There is an optional memorial: the player can bury their father
   according to the village's customs and light a lamp. This is the first "ritual" of the
   game and it unlocks the first journal entry.

## 1.4 Act I: "The Hidden Room" (early game)

- **The father's sanctum.** The cellar is larger than a cellar should be. Its first
  room holds Farrokh's spellbooks, alchemical tools, notes and the **endless bag**. A
  letter addressed to the child explains, sparingly, what Farrokh was. The letter is
  incomplete and becomes a thread for later discoveries.
- **First magic.** From the *Primer* (Farrokh's first spellbook), the player learns the
  basics of the spell grammar: *Kindle* (a small heat source), *Light* and *Lift*.
- **The carpet wakes.** A ritual of bonding: the child sleeps on the carpet. In a dream,
  the carpet "chooses" them. Afterwards the carpet responds, first by hovering, then by
  short flights. With several heirs, it chooses each of them, and the first time they
  quarrel over who steers it, it unravels into one smaller carpet per heir.
- **Learning to fly.** This is a guided flying tutorial over the valley, the orchards and
  the qanat line running into the desert.
- **Survival begins.** Food stores are burnt. The player forages, sets a first snare and
  boils water.
- **Leaving.** With the carpet, the bag and a few books, the player leaves the valley to
  follow the trail of scorched earth that Azhdar left behind.

## 1.5 Act II: "The Wide World" (the long middle, mostly open-ended)

This act is a sandbox with story threads. The player can follow it or ignore it for hours.

- **The trail of ash.** Azhdar hunts the other Great Weaves. He destroys settlements as he
  goes, and these appear as burnt, blighted places on the world map. The player finds
  survivors, rumours and clues.
- **The Circle's legacy.** The player tracks down the remaining Circle members or their
  graves, students and towers. Their homelands are spread across the generated
  cultures. Each one can:
  - teach a discipline, such as alchemy from the old herbalist in the reed marshes or
    summoning from the hermit in the mountains,
  - reveal part of the backstory,
  - hold a Great Weave, which the player can secure first, or lose to Azhdar if the player
    arrives after him.
- **Rival mages.** Independent mages roam and claim domains. Some are hostile, some trade
  and teach, and some serve Azhdar (see [Gameplay](07-gameplay.md)).
- **Revelations.**
  - Azhdar was Farrokh's closest friend and fellow Circle member.
  - The ley network is slowly dying, and magic itself is fading from the world, as it has
    been since the Age of Mana in deep time.
  - Azhdar believes that only a *reweaving* can save magic: gathering all seven Weaves
    and binding the ley network to one will, his own. He is willing to burn the world's
    people to save the world's magic.
  - Farrokh believed the Weaves had to stay apart, because one mage controlling all mana
    would be worse than a slow fading.

## 1.6 Act III: "The Reweaving" (endgame)

- Azhdar's citadel is a fortress that drifts on a ley storm. Its position is revealed by
  divination and by the Weaves the player holds.
- To reach it, the player needs mastery in several disciplines. Examples: a ritual to
  calm the storm, artifacts that protect against Azhdar's ash magic, and summoned allies.
- The final confrontation is a duel that uses the whole spell grammar and the environment
  (the citadel has its own simulated materials, fires, winds and mana fields).

### Endings (the player chooses)

| Ending | Outcome | World afterwards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unravel** | Destroy Azhdar and unravel all Weaves | Mana slowly fades over the following game years. Magic gets more expensive but people live freely |
| **Bind** | Imprison Azhdar inside his own Weave | The Weaves stay scattered. Status quo, with a prisoner who may whisper |
| **Reweave** | Perform the reweaving yourself, but share it (requires deep Ritual and Void mastery) | Ley lines are restored. The player becomes a guardian, and rival mages must be dealt with |
| **Succession** | Take Azhdar's place | The player becomes the new tyrant of mana. A dark ending, and the sandbox continues with the world reacting |

After any ending the world continues as an endless sandbox, with consequences applied to
mana fields, factions and NPC attitudes. Chronicles written after the ending tell the
player's deeds in each culture's own way.

## 1.7 How the story lives in an endless procedural world

The story is authored as a **graph of beats** in Lua. A **narrative director** anchors the
beats into the generated planet and its generated past:

- **Backstory in history.** The Circle's founding, the weaving of the Great Weaves,
  Azhdar's rise and Farrokh's years in Anarvan are written into the generated history
  database at world creation (see [World history §10.12](10-world-history.md#1012-story-integration)).
  Chronicles, NPCs and ruins across the world therefore "know" fragments of the story.
- **The home valley** is chosen at world creation. It is a semi-arid foothill oasis on a
  minor caravan road, inside a culture of the Persianate highland style family.
- **Beats, not places.** A beat declares requirements, for example "a hermit's tower in a
  mountain biome, 30–80 km from the player, not yet visited, near a ley nexus". The
  director queries the world's region index for candidate sites that match, picks one
  deterministically from the world seed, and reserves it. The region generator then places
  the authored *set piece* there, which is a parametric structure template.
- **Pacing.** Azhdar's campaign (villages burnt, Weaves taken) runs in the global macro
  simulation on a timeline that is paced by player progress. Being slow never makes the
  story fail. The world just gets darker: more ruins, more blight, more refugees on the
  roads.
- **Rumours.** NPCs, books and divination point the player toward the next beats with
  direction and distance ("far to the north-east, past the great river"). There are no
  quest markers by default. An accessibility option can enable them.
- **Determinism.** The same seed and the same choices give the same anchoring, so story
  problems are reproducible in automated tests.

## 1.8 Tone

The tone is melancholic and full of wonder, like a tale from the *Thousand and One
Nights* told by a fire that is going out. Grief is real but not grim. The world is
beautiful, dangerous and indifferent, and kindness and hospitality in it matter.
Violence against people happens off-screen or at a distance in the prologue. The retro
art style helps keep it tasteful.
