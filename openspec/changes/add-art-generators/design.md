# Design: Art Generators

## Context

The art direction is in `docs/concept/08-presentation-controls.md` §8.1: mid-90s 3D seen
through modern eyes, pixel-art textures of 32–128 px, and post-process quantisation to a
curated palette with ordered dithering. The concept also asks for procedural-first art:
"terrain and material textures, palette ramps, tile ornaments" come from generator tools
in the repository.

M0 already has four generators in `tools/gen/`. They use only the Python standard library
and a minimal deterministic PNG writer (`pngw.py`), and CI checks that `gen_all.py`
reproduces the committed files exactly. This change adds the palette, the renderer's
lookup data, the terrain materials and the first ornaments in the same style. The motivation
is in `proposal.md`, and the required behaviour is in `specs/art-generators/spec.md`.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- Stable file contracts for `add-retro-renderer`: palette, lookup table, dither matrix and
  terrain atlas.
- Byte-identical output on every platform and Python version, so the CI reproducibility
  gate holds wherever it runs.
- A palette that quantises natural terrain and sky well and carries the Oriental accents.
- Terrain materials that tile seamlessly, read at one texel per metre and stay
  distinguishable.

**Non-Goals:**

- Engine code. The renderer that loads these files is `add-retro-renderer`.
- Per-biome and per-culture palette shifts. The ramps in `base.lua` prepare them, and
  they arrive with biomes (M2) and cultures (M8).
- Vegetation sprites, architecture kit pieces and sound effects from generators. They
  come with their milestones.
- Normal maps, mipmap authoring and texture compression. The renderer builds mipmaps from
  the atlas layers at load time.

## Decisions

### 1. Generator layout

| File | Writes |
| --- | --- |
| `tools/gen/gen_palette.py` | `palettes/base.png`, `base.lua`, `base_lut.png`, `bayer8.png` |
| `tools/gen/gen_terrain.py` | `textures/terrain/atlas.png`, `atlas.lua` |
| `tools/gen/gen_ornaments.py` | `textures/ornaments/*.png` |
| `tools/gen/tilenoise.py` | (library) tileable value noise, fBm, Worley noise, domain warp, rank quantisation |
| `tools/gen/pngw.py` | (library) gains `read_png` for 8-bit RGBA files |

- Each generator has a `main(out_dir)`, like the M0 generators. `gen_all.py` calls them
  after the existing ones.
- The texture generators draw palette **indices**, not colours. A texel can only ever be a
  palette colour, and a later palette swap needs no re-quantisation.
- Generated Lua files start with the CC-BY-4.0 SPDX line and "Generated by
  tools/gen/<x>.py. Do not edit.", like `fonts/pixel.lua`.

### 2. Palette: 64 colours in 15 ramps

The palette is a list of ramps, each running from dark to light. `base.lua` stores the
ramps as 1-based indices, for palette swaps and for shading code that walks a ramp.

| Ramp | Indices | Colours (after grid snapping) | Main use |

| night | 1–4 | `080810`, `191931`, `21214a`, `3a3a6b` | Night sky, deepest shadows |
| stone | 5–10 | `292929`, `4a4242`, `635a5a`, `7b7b73`, `9c9494`, `bdbdad` | Rock, gravel, mortar, neutral greys |
| snow | 11–14 | `a5b5c5`, `c5d6de`, `e6efef`, `ffffff` | Snow and its blue shadows, cloud highlights, UI white |

| sky | 19–22 | `5284c5`, `73adde`, `a5c5ef`, `cee6f7` | Day sky, haze, ice |
| water | 23–26 | `102942`, `19425a`, `21637b`, `31849c` | Seas, lakes, puddles |
| turquoise | 27–30 | `106b6b`, `21948c`, `42bdad`, `84dece` | Accent: tiles, shallows, glacial ice |

- The large families (green, brown, stone, pomegranate) get five or six steps, because
  terrain and the carpet cover most of the screen.
- The eight mana colours (red, yellow, white, green, blue, ochre, violet, black) each map
  to a ramp.
- The closest two colours are 0.038 apart in Oklab, so no two colours are near-duplicates.
- **Grid snapping.** Every colour is snapped per channel to the lookup table's 5-bit grid,
  value v → ⌊(255·v + 15) / 31⌋. A palette colour then is exactly the centre of one
  lookup-table cell, and the table maps it to itself with distance zero. Snapping moves a
  channel by at most 4 of 255, which is invisible at this palette size.

### 3. Lookup table: nearest colour in integer Oklab

- **Layout** (the renderer's contract): 1024 × 32 RGBA, 32 slices of 32 × 32 side by side.
  Texel (x, y) holds the palette colour nearest to the colour with 5-bit levels
  r = x mod 32, g = y and b = ⌊x / 32⌋, where level v means the 8-bit value
  ⌊(255·v + 15) / 31⌋.
- **Metric:** squared Euclidean distance in Oklab (Björn Ottosson, 2020). Ties go to the
  lower palette index.
- **Why Oklab:**
  - It is perceptually more even than CIELAB in hue, so a blue sky gradient does not
    drift into violet when quantised.
  - It needs no white-point adaptation.
  - Ordered dithering adds a small offset before the lookup, so a uniform metric matters
    more than a sophisticated one.
- **Integer evaluation:**
  - The sRGB transfer function comes from a 256-entry table in 2^24 fixed point. The
    `decimal` module computes it at 40 digits, and its software arithmetic is identical
    on every platform.
  - The Oklab matrices use their published ten-digit coefficients as integers.
  - Cube roots are exact integer floor roots (Newton correction from a float guess, so
    the guess does not affect the result).
  - The components come out in 2^24 fixed point and the distances are exact integers, so
    no float rounding can flip a near-tie between platforms.
  - The unit test checks every cell against an independent floating-point Oklab. Both
    agree on all 32,768 cells; the smallest margin between the two nearest colours is
    2.7 · 10⁻⁶.
- **Alternatives considered:**
  - Weighted RGB ("redmean"): cheap, but it mishandles the blue and violet ramps.
  - CIELAB ΔE2000: better on paper, but complex and float-heavy, for no visible gain at
    64 colours with dithering.

### 4. Dither matrix

`bayer8.png` is the standard recursive 8 × 8 Bayer matrix
(M₂ₙ = [[4M, 4M+2], [4M+3, 4M+1]]). Its values are stored as 4·i + 2 in R = G = B, which
centres each of the 64 thresholds in its 1/64 band. The renderer reads one channel and
scales the offset to the palette spacing it wants.

### 5. Terrain materials

- **Contract:** `atlas.png` is 64 × (64·N) RGBA, layer i at y = 64·i. `atlas.lua` lists the
  layer names in strip order, with `size = 64` and the texture path. The order is grass,
  dry_grass, forest_floor, soil, sand, rock, gravel, snow, ice, mud, savanna, tundra. New
  materials are appended, so layer indices stay stable.
- **Seamless by construction:** every field lives on a 64 × 64 torus.
  - Value-noise lattices wrap with a period that divides 64.
  - Worley feature points are compared by wrap-around distance, searching each distinct
    cell of the 5 × 5 neighbourhood once.
  - Domain warps sample with wrap-around.
- **Scale:** one texel is about one metre, so each recipe mixes three scales: patches of
  16–32 texels, clumps of 4–8 texels and single-texel grain. Rocks are 16-texel blocks
  with cracks, gravel stones span about 4 texels, and sand ripples have a period of 8.
  Low-frequency variation stays subtle so the 64 m repeat does not stand out from the
  carpet's height.
- **Rank quantisation:** a recipe maps its scalar field to a short list of shades by
  rank. Each shade covers a fixed share of the texels, which fixes the layer's average
  colour by design. That keeps materials distinguishable (pairwise average distance at
  least 0.04 in Oklab) and makes retuning predictable.
- **Texel colours:** each layer uses 3–10 palette colours. Overlays (cracks, puddles,
  specks, moss) draw further palette indices.

### 6. Ornaments

| File | Size | Tiles along | Motif |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `star8.png` | 32 × 32 | x and y | Eight-pointed star (square ∪ diamond) whose tips touch, leaving a cross around each corner: the classic star-and-cross |
| `arabesque.png` | 64 × 64 | x and y | Gold wave stem of semicircles whose lobes curl into spirals, turquoise leaves, eight-petal rosettes on lapis |
| `kilim.png` | 32 × 32 | x and y | Hooked lozenges in 2 × 2 steps (slit-tapestry diagonals), a lattice of large and small motifs on pomegranate |
| `carpet_border.png` | 64 × 16 | x | Guard stripes and a main band of stepped medallions and small diamonds, period 16 |

- Ornaments are pure functions of the texel position on their torus. The geometry is
  integer-only: coordinates are doubled so that texel centres are odd integers, circles
  compare squared integers, and the star's diagonal uses 2·max(|u|, |v|)² ≤ r².
- No transcendental functions are needed.

### 7. Determinism

- **Values that reach an output:**
  - No `random` module, no time, no environment input.
  - Seeds are fixed integers per material.
  - Noise comes from a 32-bit integer hash of the coordinates and the seed.
  - Interpolation is integer smoothstep.
  - Exact integer square and cube roots (`math.isqrt`, the floor cube root above).
  - The `decimal` module for the sRGB table.
- **Ordering:**
  - No result depends on dict or set iteration order: sets are only sorted before use.
  - Sorts (rank quantisation) break ties by position.
  - The reproducibility test runs `gen_all.py` in a fresh interpreter with a different
    `PYTHONHASHSEED`.
- **File encoding:** the PNG writer is unchanged, filter type 0 and zlib level 9, as for the
  M0 assets. Lua files are written as UTF-8 with LF line ends on every platform.
- **Engine determinism:** the art is static content. It does not enter the replicated or
  hashed simulation state.

### 8. Tests

`tests/tools/test_art_generators.py` runs in the existing tool-test step:

- It runs `gen_all.py` into a temporary directory and compares this change's files byte
  for byte; Lua files are compared with LF line ends, because a Windows checkout may
  convert them to CRLF. The M0 assets stay covered by CI's `git diff --exit-code -- assets`
  step, since the music depends on the installed `oggenc`.
- It checks the palette, the ramps, the lookup table (every cell against a float Oklab
  reference), the dither matrix, the atlas contract, the tiling, the average colours,
  palette-only texels, the credits, the Lua headers and the memory budget.
- **Tiling check:** each wrap-around edge is compared with the interior neighbours.
  - Each line is first averaged over 7 texels along the edge, so single-texel grain does
    not hide a low-frequency seam.
  - The seam may not exceed 1.25 × the largest or 2 × the mean interior difference.
  - The committed layers score at most 0.93 and 1.35 on these two ratios. A layer whose
    lightness drifts by 0.19 across the tile scores 2.5–7.1 and 3.8–12.5 and is caught;
    a test proves that on the grass layer.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **zlib output could change between zlib versions** → the M0 assets carry the same risk,
  and CI regenerates on a pinned Ubuntu image. If a zlib update ever changes the bytes,
  the CI diff shows it and the assets are regenerated in one commit.
- **The palette is a taste decision** → it is data at the top of `gen_palette.py`. Changing
  a colour regenerates the lookup table and textures automatically, and the tests keep the
  structural rules (ramps, distinctness, grid) intact.
- **64 colours may band in large sky gradients** → the ordered dither covers the gaps, and
  the sky ramps (night, lapis, sky, snow) leave no lightness gap larger than 0.13. If
  banding shows in `add-retro-renderer`, the renderer can quantise the sky with a
  dedicated ramp.
- **One texture per material repeats every 64 m** → the renderer can break the repetition
  with world-space variation (macro noise, blending between layers). The textures keep
  low frequencies subtle for that reason.

## Open Questions

- Should biomes get palette variants (for example a warmer desert palette) as separate
  `palettes/*.png` with their own lookup tables, or as ramp remaps of `base`? This is
  decided with `add-climate-and-biomes` in M2.
